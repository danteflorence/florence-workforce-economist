# Top 10 objections

> _What you'll hear. What to say back. Keep this open during your first
> 20 calls. By call 40 you'll have your own version._

---

## 1. "We've tried international hiring before. It didn't go well."

**What's behind it:** Usually one of two stories — a one-off visa
debacle, or a culture/retention failure where the international hires
left within a year.

**What to say:**
> "Tell me what went wrong — I want to know if it was the visa side or
> the integration side, because those are different problems with
> different solutions. Either way, we run the cohort model precisely
> because one-off international hires are brittle. Your facility
> doesn't onboard a single nurse — you onboard a cohort of 6 to 12 who
> arrived together, trained together, and are studying for their next
> certs together."

**Don't say:** "That won't happen with us." It will sound dismissive of
their real experience.

---

## 2. "What does the union think?"

**What's behind it:** A specific stakeholder management problem, not a
philosophical objection. They need a script for their union meeting.

**What to say:**
> "Most of the systems we work with are union, including some of your
> peer systems. The framing that has landed: Florence-placed RNs are
> paid prevailing wage or above, they're permanent FTEs on the same
> contract as everyone else, and they replace agency contractors — not
> existing union members. The data we have is: union RN headcount goes
> up at our customer sites in year 1, because we displace agency
> dependence and let them re-open units."

**Have ready:** the standard union-conversation FAQ from your account
team.

---

## 3. "Our internal recruiting team would prefer to do this themselves."

**What's behind it:** Internal politics. The TA director feels
threatened. The right response is to make the TA director the hero.

**What to say:**
> "Your team isn't being replaced. They'd own the in-house pipeline,
> we'd own the international one. The international RN sourcing,
> credentialing, and visa work is full-time skilled labor that nobody
> has on their TA team. We hand the nurse off, fully cleared, and your
> team does what they do best: onboarding and integration."

**Then:** ask to meet with the TA director directly. Make the case
that this expands their headcount story, not threatens it.

---

## 4. "Your data is from 2023. The market has changed."

**What's behind it:** They're testing your sophistication. This is
actually a buying signal.

**What to say:**
> "You're right — HCRIS is FY23 data with the standard 12-to-18-month
> lag. That's why our pricing engine also pulls live BLS JOLTS,
> CES, and OEWS for the current-quarter view. The HCRIS data gives us
> the facility-specific baseline; BLS gives us the trend. If you'd
> share even one quarter of your current internals, we'd plug those in
> and re-quote in 48 hours. That's actually the conversation I'd love
> to upgrade us to."

**The objection is the doorway** to a data-sharing partnership. Don't
defend; invite.

---

## 5. "Your fee is too high."

**What's behind it:** Anchor moved. They have an alternative quote (often
from a non-Florence visa pathway broker offering "just" $30K).

**What to say:**
> "What's the comparison? If they're quoting $30K, they're quoting a
> placement transaction. We're quoting a 24-month relationship: the
> nurse, their visa, their credentialing, their portal, their
> mentorship, our reporting, and the replacement guarantee. If you took
> our fee apart, the actual placement-and-visa portion is roughly
> comparable. The rest of the fee is what gets you retention."

**Then:** "What would actually be the right price for the outcome
you're trying to buy?" — gets them to articulate the outcome, not the
unit cost.

---

## 6. "What if the nurse leaves in year 1?"

**What's behind it:** Real risk; they have lived through it.

**What to say:**
> "If the nurse leaves before month 12, you get a replacement or a
> pro-rata refund. Our retention data shows median tenure of
> <TENURE>+ months — multiples longer than agency. But the contract is
> the floor. Our portal, our cohort model, and our mentor program are
> the reason the data is what it is, not the contract clause."

**Show them:** the retention number from your existing customers and
the nurse portal demo.

---

## 7. "How long until the nurse is actually on our floor?"

**What's behind it:** Capacity planning. They're trying to figure out
if Florence helps with Q3 or with next fiscal year.

**What to say:**
> "Realistically, 6 to 9 months from contract to badge. The credentialing
> and visa packet runs in parallel with the source-and-screen phase. We
> won't promise faster — that's the realistic window. If you need
> Q3 coverage, you should keep your bridge plan running and use us to
> retire it by Q1 next year."

**Don't promise:** specific dates. The cohort calendar is real and
delivery dates depend on the visa pipeline you do not control.

---

## 8. "We don't want our patients to feel like they're at a 'foreign
   nurse' hospital."

**What's behind it:** Usually said clumsily but the underlying concern
is real: community perception, complaint risk, marketing.

**What to say:**
> "Your patients won't notice — they'll meet RNs who are credentialed,
> oriented, and on your team. The percentage of internationally-trained
> RNs in the US workforce has been roughly 15% for decades — your
> patients already meet them every day. What our customers tell us
> after 12 months is that the conversation went from 'will this be
> noticed?' to 'why didn't we do this 5 years ago?'"

**Don't:** lecture them. Acknowledge the concern, address the data,
move on.

---

## 9. "We're in a hiring freeze."

**What's behind it:** Could be real, could be a soft no.

**What to say:**
> "The credentialing and visa pipeline is 6 to 9 months. We can start
> the work on a paid retainer now so the freeze doesn't cost you the
> Q1 cohort. If the freeze lifts and you don't need them, we'd find
> them another home — we have a waiting list of operators in your
> region. If the freeze is real and long-term, let's talk again in 90
> days; I'll keep your data set warm."

**Then:** ask when their next budget cycle locks. Plant a calendar
flag.

---

## 10. "Send us a proposal and we'll get back to you."

**What's behind it:** Either real interest or polite dismissal. You
need to figure out which.

**What to say:**
> "Happy to. Before I do — what's the decision process on your side?
> Who else needs to see this? When do you typically make a decision
> like this? That'll help me tailor the proposal to what your team
> actually needs."

**If they answer with specifics:** real. Send the proposal.

**If they answer with vagueness:** polite dismissal. Send a smaller
artifact — a one-pager with the headline numbers — and ask for 20
minutes to walk through it.
