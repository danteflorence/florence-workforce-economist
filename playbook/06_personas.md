# Buyer personas

> _The four people who decide whether Florence happens at a health
> system. Know what each cares about. Know what proves it._

---

## The CFO

**What they're optimizing for:** the nursing labor line on the P&L,
specifically the contract-labor sub-line that has been ugly since 2020.

**What keeps them up at night:**
- Quarterly board reviews where the CFO has to explain why contract
  labor is still 18% of nursing payroll
- Capital projects on hold because they can't model labor cost
- The hospital's bond rating; the rating agency conversations

**What proves it to them:**
- The proposal Excel, page-by-facility savings
- Provenance traceback — "this is your FY23 cost report"
- Multi-year savings projection
- The methodology section (yes, they will read it)

**What you say first:**
> "I'm not going to make you guess at the savings. The Excel has every
> facility broken out with the math shown."

**What you don't say:**
- "Trust us, the savings are real" — they don't and they shouldn't
- "Most of our customers see..." — they don't care about other
  hospitals; they care about theirs
- Anything about the FICA mechanic — wrong audience for that detail

**Where they read the playbook:** the methodology section, twice.

---

## The CNO (Chief Nursing Officer)

**What they're optimizing for:** unit-level quality, retention, and
their relationship with their nursing managers.

**What keeps them up at night:**
- Unit managers quitting because they can't staff
- Travelers walking off mid-contract
- A bad survey because the unit was understaffed during inspection
- Their own credibility — they have promised relief and not delivered

**What proves it to them:**
- The nurse value-prop story (chapter 03)
- The retention number from existing customers
- The portal demo — "here's what your nurses will use"
- A reference call with a peer CNO

**What you say first:**
> "I want to show you the nurses, not just the numbers. Can I walk you
> through the portal first?"

**What you don't say:**
- "We'll handle it" — they have heard that from every vendor for a
  decade
- "Our nurses are NCLEX-ready" — table stakes; lead with the career
  and community story instead
- Anything that sounds like staffing-agency vocabulary ("bill rate,"
  "shift differential")

**Where they read the playbook:** the nurse value-prop section, the
retention discussion in the operator value-prop section.

---

## Talent Acquisition / HR

**What they're optimizing for:** time-to-fill, regulatory compliance,
not getting blamed when something goes wrong.

**What keeps them up at night:**
- Open reqs older than 120 days
- Visa-sponsorship paperwork (their team probably handles 5–50 sponsorships a year
  and is overworked)
- Discrimination complaints if an international hire goes badly
- Onboarding capacity — orientation classes filling up

**What proves it to them:**
- The compliance packet — visa documentation, NCLEX, state license
  verification, English proficiency
- The replacement guarantee terms
- A clean implementation playbook ("here's what we hand off to your
  team")
- Their workload going down, not up

**What you say first:**
> "We'd own the visa, the credentialing, and the English proficiency
> documentation. You'd run our nurses through your standard orientation,
> same as any new hire."

**What you don't say:**
- Nothing about volume that sounds aggressive ("we can do 200 nurses
  next year"); they hear that as "200 onboarding sessions"
- Nothing that suggests they wouldn't be in the loop on compliance

**Where they read the playbook:** the operator value-prop section, the
objection list ("are we going to take heat?").

---

## The COO / VP Operations

**What they're optimizing for:** operational continuity, service-line
performance, the operating margin.

**What keeps them up at night:**
- Closed units they can't reopen because of staffing
- Service-line revenue down because OR is dark on weekends
- Joint Commission re-survey risk
- The CEO asking "why is our cost of care so high?"

**What proves it to them:**
- The capacity-expansion economics (Care Settings tab)
- The forecast tab — labor-market trajectory
- The market-intelligence picture for their competitive set

**What you say first:**
> "What service line would you grow if you had 10 more permanent RNs?"

**What you don't say:**
- Anything that sounds like a staffing agency — they hate them
- "We'll save you money" — they're not buying on price alone

**Where they read the playbook:** the market intelligence summary; the
deal flow.

---

## A note on the CEO

You will rarely sell directly to a CEO. They will be briefed by the CFO
and the CNO. Your job is to give those two people the artifacts they
need to brief their boss credibly.

If you DO get face time with a CEO:
- 90 seconds, headline savings + headline retention
- The two-page exec summary in their hand
- Specific facilities named — "your Oakland and Sacramento facilities
  are the largest opportunity"
- Then get out of the way and let the CFO take it from there
