# Value to nurses

> _Reps don't usually pitch this part. Why is it in the playbook? Because
> every operator buyer asks "why do nurses pick you?" and every nurse who
> hears your pitch will ask "what's in it for me?" You should be able to
> answer both._

## The headline message to a nurse

**"You came into this profession to take care of people. We make the
career around that profession actually work — in the United States, on
your terms."**

Florence is the only US RN placement platform that is **nurse-first**
end to end. That's not a tagline; it's a business decision. Every
operator complaint we have ever had ("you guys are too pro-nurse") is a
feature, not a bug.

---

## What a nurse actually gets

### 1. A real US RN career, not a one-shift visa hop

A Florence-placed nurse gets a permanent position at a real US health
system, not a per-diem contract that ends when the visa does. The
career stretches out across years. The portal helps them see where it
goes:

- RN → Charge Nurse → CNS / NP / CRNA timelines and wage milestones
- Specialty premium data (ICU vs. Med/Surg vs. OR)
- Certification pathways (CCRN, CEN, etc.) with study groups

### 2. Wage benchmarking they can actually use

Every Florence nurse has a personalized wage view in the portal:

- Their current wage vs. their MSA's prevailing wage
- "If you moved to <STATE>, you'd earn <DELTA> more per year"
- Specialty premiums — what the next step on their specialty ladder pays
- All sourced from the same BLS data we sell to operators

This is **the** retention tool. A nurse who knows what they're worth and
what their next step pays is a nurse who plans their career inside the
US, not back home.

### 3. A community of peers

Florence cohorts move together. The Q3 2024 PHI cohort, for example,
has its own group in the portal — they were credentialed together,
arrived together, are working at sister facilities, are studying for
CCRN together. Birthdays, milestones, first-day photos, weekly check-ins.

For a nurse who has just relocated from the Philippines to a tertiary
hospital in California, this community is not a feature — it is the
difference between staying and leaving.

### 4. Mentorship

Senior Florence nurses become mentors to incoming cohorts. We pair them
on specialty (an ICU nurse with 4 years US experience mentors a new ICU
nurse on visa). Mentors are paid for the time. Mentees get a real human
to ask, "Is this normal?"

### 5. The Florence promise — quietly

We don't write this on the website. We say it in person:

> _"Your career is yours. Your license is yours. Your family
> reunification process is yours. We are the company that handles the
> paperwork and gets out of your way."_

Every operational decision Florence has made — the portal, the cohort
groups, the wage benchmarking, the milestone celebrations — exists
because we believe nurses who feel ownership of their career stay in it
longer. Operators get retention. We get a brand. Nurses get a life.

## Why this matters for the operator pitch

When a CNO asks, "How is your retention so much better than agency?"
the answer is not "we screen better." The answer is:

> **"We give the nurse a career, not a contract. We give them tools to
> see what they're worth. We give them a community. We follow them for
> 24+ months. None of that is what an agency does — agencies optimize
> for utilization. We optimize for the nurse's own next 5 years."**

This is the answer that closes CNOs. They have lived through agency
turnover. They know exactly what we're describing.

---

## What we tell nurses about the operator

We tell them the truth about the math:

> _The hospital is paying a real placement fee for you. They have skin
> in the game on your success. If you stay — and you have every reason
> to — they win. If you leave early, they lose. That alignment is why we
> only place at hospitals that take you seriously._

This is also why we won't place at facilities we don't believe in. The
manual-review flag in the pricing engine is not just a data signal —
it's also where we filter out hospitals that have an HR or culture
problem we've heard about from prior cohorts.

---

## What we will NOT say to a nurse

- We will not tell a nurse that their visa pathway is easy. It is real
  work. We respect them by being honest.
- We will not tell a nurse what they "should" specialize in. We give
  them data; they choose.
- We will not say "you're like family." They have a family. We are
  their employer's placement partner and their career platform. Real,
  helpful, professional — not performative.
