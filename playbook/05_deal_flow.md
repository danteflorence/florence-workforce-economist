# The deal flow

> _Five stages from cold list to signed agreement. The Workbench tab
> mirrors these stages. Move a deal forward only when the gate condition
> is met._

```
PROSPECT  →  DISCOVERY  →  PROPOSAL  →  REVIEW  →  CLOSED
   |            |            |            |          |
 first       qualified    proposal     leadership   signed /
 contact     +           generated     review +     refused
             discovery   + sent        approval
             notes
             logged
```

---

## Stage 1 — Prospect

**Goal:** Get the meeting.

**What you're doing:**
- Pulling the target list from the lead-scoring tab
- Running the AI sales-brief for the top systems
- Crafting an outreach email tied to a specific local data point

**Your tools:**
- `Lead scoring` tab — highest Florence-fit scores
- `Health systems` tab — facility-level view
- `Sales brief` agent — auto-generated talking points
- `Market intelligence` tab — what changed for this system in the last
  30 days

**The outreach formula:**
1. Lead with a specific number from their own facilities
2. Reference a recent event (news, M&A, layoffs, ratings change)
3. Ask for 20 minutes to walk their numbers

**Gate to next stage:** Discovery call booked.

---

## Stage 2 — Discovery

**Goal:** Understand their reality. Validate the pricing-engine
assumptions against their actuals.

**What you're doing:**
- Discovery call (30–45 min)
- Listening for: current agency spend, top-3 understaffed units,
  retention pain, growth plans, CMS rating concerns
- Logging notes in the Workbench

**Discovery question library:**

> **Frame:** "Before I show you any numbers, I want to make sure I'm
> solving the right problem. Mind if I ask a few things?"

1. "How many contract or agency RNs are you running across the system
   right now? Across which facilities?"
2. "What's your average bill rate today? Has it moved this year?"
3. "Which units feel it the most — ICU? OR? Med/Surg? Float pool?"
4. "When you've tried to convert agency RNs to FTE, what's been hard?"
5. "What does your CFO see when they look at your nursing labor line?
   Is it trending the way they want?"
6. "Are there service lines you'd open or expand if you had permanent
   nursing capacity?"
7. "What's your Joint Commission cycle look like?"
8. "Has your system tried international hiring before? What was the
   experience?"

**Bad discovery signals:** they want the deck before the call, they
won't say what they're paying, they push every question off to "our
CFO will need to weigh in."

**Good discovery signals:** they tell you a number; they name a unit;
they ask "how would this work for us?"

**Gate to next stage:** Discovery notes logged. CFO or CNO has agreed
to see a proposal.

---

## Stage 3 — Proposal

**Goal:** Put numbers in front of them that they cannot ignore.

**What you're doing:**
- Generating the proposal bundle in the Workbench
- Customizing the cover note with their language from discovery
- Sending the proposal (Excel + 2-page exec summary PDF)
- Scheduling the follow-up call

**What the bundle contains:**
- **Page 1** — Headline savings + capacity reclaimed (the CFO slide)
- **Page 2** — Methodology + provenance (the audit-trail slide)
- **Excel workbook** — Per-facility tab, 3-tier pricing bands,
  amortization schedule

**Sending it:**
- Send to the most senior name you have a relationship with
- CC the CNO if they were in the discovery call
- Don't BCC; this is a serious tool, not a marketing email

**What you say in the email body:**
> "Attached is the savings analysis we walked through. Page 1 is the
> headline; page 2 explains the math. The Excel has every facility
> broken out so your team can validate. I'd like to hold the follow-up
> on <DATE> — does <TIME> work?"

**Gate to next stage:** Customer has acknowledged receipt and either
booked a follow-up or sent specific questions.

---

## Stage 4 — Review

**Goal:** Surface objections, defend the math, get to terms.

**What you're doing:**
- Follow-up call with whoever is reviewing
- Working through objection list (see chapter 07)
- Offering the data-share upgrade if they push on accuracy
- Looping in sales leadership for ANY material concession

**What requires sales leadership sign-off:**
- Any price below the "Reference" tier band
- Any non-standard contract clause (replacement guarantee, ramp-down,
  performance-based pricing)
- Any commitment to a specific cohort calendar (we don't promise
  delivery dates we can't keep)

**The Workbench has a "Send to leadership for review" button** that
locks the proposal at its current state and notifies sales leadership.
Use it before you negotiate, not after.

**Gate to next stage:** Customer says yes to terms, or says no with a
specific reason.

---

## Stage 5 — Closed

**Two terminal states:**

- **Closed-won** — contract signed; account moves to ops. You stay on
  the relationship as the named account contact for the first 12 months.
- **Closed-lost** — log the reason. Patterns matter for the playbook.
  Common reasons we'll want to track: "internal hiring freeze," "chose
  agency partner," "international hiring stigma," "not ready to commit
  to 36mo," "price."

**Post-close handoff:**
- Schedule the kickoff with the customer's HR + clinical leadership
- Brief the cohort team on the customer's specific needs (specialties,
  facility list, start-date target)
- Set the first quarterly business review (90 days out)

---

## How long should each stage take?

> _This is a guideline, not a quota. Some health systems move in 2
> weeks; some move in 9 months. Don't force the cycle._

| Stage     | Typical | Red flag if longer than |
|-----------|---------|-------------------------|
| Prospect  | 2-4 wks | 8 wks with no response  |
| Discovery | 1-2 wks | 4 wks                   |
| Proposal  | 1 wk    | 3 wks                   |
| Review    | 2-6 wks | 12 wks                  |

A deal stuck in Review for 12+ weeks is usually a "no" that hasn't been
said out loud. Push for the no — it frees you up.
