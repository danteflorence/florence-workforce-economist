> a buyer. Pull the actual numbers from the proposal Excel — the framing

## The headline message

**"We turn your premium agency spend into permanent capacity. Same
hours, same coverage, lower cost, higher retention."**

Every other line in this section unpacks one half of that sentence.

## The four things they actually get

### 1. Lower cost, durably

Your hospital pays $X to an agency for an RN hour today. A permanent RN
in your MSA costs $Y per hour fully loaded. The gap is your **agency
premium** — and it isn't a one-time expense. You're paying it every
shift, every week, every quarter that you're short.

Florence converts the gap into a one-time placement fee, amortized over
36 months. After year 1, the nurse is just a nurse on your payroll. You
have re-priced a recurring expense as a capex-style outlay.

**What to say:** "Today you're renting nursing capacity at premium. We
let you own it at market."

### 2. Permanent capacity, not staffing

Agencies optimize for utilization of THEIR book. They will rotate your
shift to a different hospital if it bids more, and they will not staff
your weekend if their margin is too thin. Florence-placed RNs are
**your** RNs. They are on your payroll, in your scrub colors, on your
on-call rotation.

This matters most for:
- **Service-line growth.** You cannot open a new OR or expand an ED
  without permanent staff. Agency RNs can't carry continuity-of-care.
- **Quality scores.** Hospital-acquired-condition rates correlate with
  permanent staff ratios.
- **Joint Commission and state survey readiness.** Permanent staff know
  your protocols.

**What to say:** "We're not asking you to staff with us — we're asking
you to hire with us."

### 3. Retention by design

International RNs placed by Florence have median tenure of **<TENURE>**
months at the placing facility — multiples longer than agency contract
length, longer than the US-trained RN national average for the first 24
months on a unit.

Why: the nurse has chosen this career, this country, this employer. They
have a Florence career portal that maps the next 5 years of their
US career. They have a community of cohort peers. The friction to leave
is high.

**What to say:** "We don't optimize for placement volume. We optimize
for the nurse staying with you for years."

### 4. A real workforce planning tool

Every Florence customer gets access to the same surveillance data we
use internally:

- BLS JOLTS healthcare openings, hires, quits, layoffs — refreshed
  monthly
- BLS OEWS RN wages by state and MSA
- BLS CES healthcare employment
- Forecast: 12-month projection of healthcare labor tightness
- News feed: M&A, layoffs, openings in your competitive set

You're not just buying nurses. You're buying the intelligence to plan
around them.

**What to say:** "When you hire Florence, you also hire our market
intelligence."

## The three things they're afraid of

> _Know these before you sit down. The objection chapter has full
> responses; here's what's running through their head before they say
> anything out loud._

1. **"Will the nurse be ready to work my floor on day one?"** Yes, with
   the credentialing already complete. Florence-placed RNs come with
   NCLEX, state license, English proficiency, and US clinical
   orientation already done. You will give them your facility
   orientation; we have given them everything else.

2. **"What happens if it doesn't work out?"** The placement fee is
   amortized over 36 months. If the nurse leaves before month 12, you
   get a replacement or a pro-rata refund — terms vary by package; see
   the customer contract. We have skin in the game.

3. **"Are we going to take heat for hiring international nurses?"** No.
   US health systems hire internationally-credentialed RNs every day —
   it's a normal part of the labor market. Our nurses are paid
   prevailing wage or above. We can help you communicate this
   internally; ask your account team for the standard FAQ.

## The two stories not to lead with

> _These are real, but they are not the way in. Save them for the
> follow-up meeting, never the first._

- **The tax / FICA angle.** Real, internal, do not lead with it. This is
  how WE think about pricing. The customer thinks about savings and
  capacity. See the glossary entry for FICA offset for context, but
  treat it as an internal mechanic only.
- **The visa pathway details.** Real, complex, do not lead with it. The
  customer cares that the nurse is legally cleared to work, not which
  visa class they hold. Your account team handles the immigration
  packet.
